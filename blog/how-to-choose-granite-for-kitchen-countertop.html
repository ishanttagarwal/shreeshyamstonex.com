Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Granite for Your Kitchen Countertop

By Shree Shyam Stonex 10 June 2025 9 min read

Choosing kitchen granite is part design decision, part engineering. The slab you pick will face hot pans, acidic juices, knives, and twenty years of daily wear. This guide walks you through every variable that matters — in plain language.

1. Start with how you actually cook

If you cook daily with oil, turmeric and dark spices, prioritise dense, low-porosity granite like Jet Black, Coin Black Lapatro or Jeerawal White. These reject stains better than lighter exotic granites with open mineral patterns.

If your kitchen is more aesthetic than functional, exotic patterned stones like Tiger Brown or Bagera Brown deliver dramatic visual impact and can be sealed periodically.

2. Understand porosity and absorption

Granite’s water absorption rate (measured per ASTM C97) typically ranges from 0.05% to 0.40%. Lower is better for kitchens.

  • < 0.10% — Excellent for kitchens (Jet Black, Coin Black)
  • 0.10% – 0.25% — Good for kitchens with sealing (Jeerawal White, P. White)
  • 0.25% – 0.40% — Best for low-use areas, vanities, feature walls

3. Slab thickness: 18mm vs 20mm vs 30mm

Indian kitchens typically use 18mm or 20mm slabs. The 30mm slabs are reserved for cantilevered breakfast bars or premium projects where the visible edge is part of the design.

Thicker is not automatically better — a properly laminated 18mm slab with mitred edges can look identical to 30mm at a fraction of the freight cost. Important when exporting to Nepal, the Middle East, or Europe.

4. Edge profiles — the underrated decision

Six edges cover 95% of kitchen projects:

  1. Eased (Square): Modern, minimal, easy to clean.
  2. Half-Bullnose: Soft, family-friendly, hides chips.
  3. Full Bullnose: Traditional, gentle on hips.
  4. Ogee: Classical European look, premium feel.
  5. Bevel: Sharp, contemporary, light-catching.
  6. Mitred (Laminated): Creates illusion of 40–60mm thickness.

5. Finish matters more than colour

The same granite block can look completely different in polished, honed, leathered or flamed finishes. For kitchens we recommend:

  • Polished — reflective, easiest to clean, shows water marks on dark stones.
  • Leathered (Lapatro) — satin-soft, hides fingerprints, ideal for black granite islands.
  • Honed — matte, contemporary, requires more frequent sealing.

6. Indian vs imported granite

India is the world’s second-largest granite exporter, and Kishangarh, Rajasthan is its processing capital. Brazilian or Italian granites add 30–120% to your cost in freight and import duty without measurably superior performance. For most Indian and South Asian projects, Kishangarh-processed Indian stone is the rational choice.

7. Verify before you buy

Always ask your supplier for:

  • Block-matching slab photos (not catalogue images)
  • Lot/bundle number for batch consistency
  • Test report (ASTM C97 absorption, C170 compressive strength)
  • Edge sample and finish sample — physical, not digital

The shortcut

If you want a single safe recommendation for an Indian kitchen built for two decades of hard use: Jeerawal White for a bright kitchen, Coin Black Lapatro for a moody contemporary one. Both stones are quarried and processed in Kishangarh, both have proven 20-year track records, and both ship globally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which granite is best for an Indian kitchen?

Jet Black, Coin Black Lapatro, and Jeerawal White are the most reliable choices for Indian kitchens because of their low porosity and resistance to turmeric and oil stains.

How thick should a kitchen granite slab be?

18mm or 20mm is standard. 30mm is used for cantilevered overhangs or premium designs. Mitred 18mm slabs can visually mimic 40–60mm thickness.

How often does kitchen granite need sealing?

Polished dense granite typically needs sealing once every 12–18 months. Honed or leathered finishes require sealing every 6–12 months.
